# Ethical Guidelines for Counter-Disinformation Work

## Core Principles

This system is designed to protect truth and democratic values, not to manipulate public opinion. All use must adhere to these principles:

### 1. Transparency

**Always be transparent about who you are and what you're doing.**

- ✅ **DO**: Clearly identify yourself and your organization
- ✅ **DO**: Disclose if content is part of a coordinated effort
- ✅ **DO**: Provide sources for all claims
- ❌ **DON'T**: Impersonate other individuals or organizations
- ❌ **DON'T**: Hide your identity or affiliation
- ❌ **DON'T**: Use fake accounts or personas

### 2. Truth-Based Only

**Only share information you have verified as accurate.**

- ✅ **DO**: Fact-check all claims before sharing
- ✅ **DO**: Cite credible sources
- ✅ **DO**: Correct your own mistakes immediately
- ❌ **DON'T**: Share unverified information
- ❌ **DON'T**: Exaggerate or distort facts
- ❌ **DON'T**: Use misleading framing

### 3. Platform Compliance

**Respect all platform Terms of Service.**

- ✅ **DO**: Follow rate limits and API terms
- ✅ **DO**: Respect robots.txt and scraping policies
- ✅ **DO**: Report violations through proper channels
- ❌ **DON'T**: Use automation to violate ToS
- ❌ **DON'T**: Engage in vote manipulation
- ❌ **DON'T**: Create fake engagement

### 4. Human-Centered

**AI assists, humans decide.**

- ✅ **DO**: Have humans review all content before posting
- ✅ **DO**: Use AI for analysis and recommendations
- ✅ **DO**: Empower real people with better tools
- ❌ **DON'T**: Auto-post AI-generated content
- ❌ **DON'T**: Replace human judgment with algorithms
- ❌ **DON'T**: Use bots to simulate grassroots movements

### 5. No Manipulation

**Focus on education and facts, not propaganda.**

- ✅ **DO**: Educate people about manipulation tactics
- ✅ **DO**: Provide context and nuance
- ✅ **DO**: Engage in good-faith discussion
- ❌ **DON'T**: Use emotional manipulation
- ❌ **DON'T**: Employ disinformation tactics yourself
- ❌ **DON'T**: Suppress legitimate speech

## Operational Guidelines

### When to Respond

**Respond when:**
- False information is spreading rapidly
- Public health or safety is at risk
- Democratic processes are being undermined
- Verified facts can counter the narrative

**Don't respond when:**
- It would amplify a marginal narrative
- You don't have verified facts
- Engagement would be counterproductive
- The issue is opinion/values, not facts

### How to Respond

**Effective responses:**
- Lead with facts, not by repeating the false claim
- Provide alternative explanations
- Use credible messengers
- Make it easy to verify
- Focus on inoculation (prebunking)

**Avoid:**
- Directly quoting false claims (avoids backfire effect)
- Engaging in heated arguments
- Personal attacks
- Defensive or condescending tone

### Content Review Process

**All content must go through review:**

1. **Draft**: AI recommendations or human-written
2. **Fact-check**: Verify all claims
3. **Legal review**: Ensure no defamation/violations
4. **Approval**: Human approver signs off
5. **Attribution**: Clearly attribute source
6. **Publish**: Share with transparency

### Platform Reporting

**When reporting to platforms:**

- Provide clear evidence of violations
- Document coordinated behavior
- Respect platform processes
- Don't abuse reporting systems
- Follow up appropriately

## Legal Considerations

### What's Legal

- Monitoring public posts
- Sharing factual information
- Organizing legitimate responses
- Reporting ToS violations

### What's Potentially Problematic

- Accessing private information
- Harassment or brigading
- Defamation
- Copyright violations

**When in doubt, consult legal counsel.**

## Measuring Success

**Good metrics:**
- Truth reaches more people than lies
- Narratives slow after intervention
- People report increased media literacy
- Platforms remove coordinated campaigns

**Bad metrics:**
- Volume of content posted
- Suppression of opposing views
- "Winning" arguments
- Silencing critics

## Accountability

### Regular Reviews

- Monthly ethics review of operations
- Quarterly external audit
- Annual public transparency report

### Incident Response

If ethical violations occur:
1. Immediately stop problematic activity
2. Document what happened
3. Notify affected parties
4. Implement corrective measures
5. Public acknowledgment if warranted

### Feedback Mechanisms

- Regular team discussions
- External ethics board
- Public feedback channel
- Whistleblower protection

## Red Lines

**Immediately stop and reassess if:**

- You're considering deception
- You're hiding your identity
- You're using tactics you criticize
- You're operating in legal grey areas
- Team members express ethical concerns
- You're prioritizing "winning" over truth

## Remember

**The goal is not to "defeat" the other side—it's to protect truth and democratic discourse.**

You're playing the long game. Credibility is your most valuable asset. One scandal can undo years of good work.

When facing disinformation campaigns that don't follow rules, your ethical constraints are not a weakness—they're your strategic advantage.

**Trust takes years to build and seconds to destroy. Protect it fiercely.**

## Questions to Ask

Before any action, ask:

1. Is this true?
2. Is it transparent?
3. Does it respect people's autonomy?
4. Would I be comfortable if this became public?
5. Am I building trust or eroding it?

If you can't answer "yes" to all of these, reconsider the action.
